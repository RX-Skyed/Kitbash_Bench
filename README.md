# Kitbash Bench

A workbench in your browser for putting together 3D-printable miniatures. Load STL files, snap the parts together, pose them, and export a single print-ready model, or lay everything out as a model kit on sprues.

Nothing to install, and nothing is uploaded: your files stay on your computer.

---

## What it can do

- **Basic shapes:** add a **Cube**, **Sphere**, **Pyramid** or **Cylinder** from the **Shapes** tile (Files or Assemble) or File › Add a shape…. It lands on the mat beside the selected part, sized to suit it, and works like any other part (snap it, magnet it, crack it, export it). Properties sets its width, depth and height in mm, and the sides of a pyramid (3 triangular, 4 square, more for a cone) or cylinder (6 for a hex prism, 8 an octagon), or the smoothness of a sphere. Once it has modifiers its size is fixed.
- **Snap parts together:** click the faces, balls, sockets or surfaces that meet, and the bench fits them: pegs into holes, ball joints into cups, notched surfaces into each other.
- **Do ya feel lucky?:** click two parts and nothing else. The bench tries every way one could sit on the other and puts it where the most surface sits flush with nothing overlapping. Step through its other guesses if the first isn't right. It also judges how well a fit **locks in**: a peg in its socket is held every way but out, while a part resting on a curve or nestled against a wall is only held one way, so keyed joints (a hex peg in a backpack's slot, a tab in its socket) win over places a part merely touches a lot. Poses that land a fraction of a millimetre or a few degrees off a socket and jam against its walls are seated with a grid of small turns and shifts before they're judged. Guesses that lock in say so.
- **Pose figures:** snapped parts become a jointed skeleton. Turn an arm, head or weapon at its joint and everything attached follows.
- **Connection types:** each link is **Pinned** (turns any way, the default), **Hinge** (turns about one axis, with optional angle limits), **Roller** (slides along one of the part's own axes, with optional travel limits, and turns any way as it goes, like a ball riding in a channel) or **Fixed**. A hinge, roller or rail can also take its direction from faces you pick: **Pick faces…**, click the faces (a groove's walls, a flat strip), then **Along**, **Across** or **Out**.
- **Pistons and linkages:** a part can also be pinned to a second part, or ride a rail on it, as well as hanging off its parent. Make one with the snap tools: snap a part that's already on a joint (a piston pinned in its socket) to a second part and choose **Slide in it** (the default) or **Pin to it too** in Adjust. It turns on its own joint to reach, then rides a rail down the channel you snapped it to, with the rail's direction and ends read from the channel itself. Pose the leg and the pistons turn and slide to stay connected; slide a piston and the leg bends to match. If a piston can't reach, the pose stops where the real model would jam.
- **Link in place** (was Auto-assemble): for files exported already in their assembled places. Nothing moves: the bench finds where the parts touch and joins them there, with joints, for you. Before it builds, you can change what hangs off what, joint or fixed, and which parts are alternates of each other.
- **Reference images:** drop a picture on the view (or View › Add reference image…). It faces your view, behind the parts; size it, make it see-through, move and turn it to line it up, and pose the model against it. **Match pose** (experimental) bends the joints until the model's outline covers the figure in the picture.
- **Part axes and origin, like Blender:** every part has its own X, Y and Z and an origin (the point it turns about). Switch **Affect** in the Move/Rotate bar to **Axes & origin** (Blender's Affect Only Origins), or use **Edit axes** in Properties: then **Rotate (R)** turns the part's axes and **Move (G)** moves its origin, and the part itself stays put. Properties › **Axes & origin** also takes typed angles, **Square to world** (like Apply rotation), **From faces…** (click faces and Z runs along, across or straight out of them, with the origin on them), **Origin to middle** and **Reset**. With **Axes: Part** the arrows follow the edited axes, Rotate turns the part about its origin, and rails set to the part's X, Y or Z use them too. The mesh is never changed, so exports are the same.
- **Section view:** press **K** (or View › Section view, or the **Section view** tile in Assemble and Prepare) and a cutting plane appears through what's selected (along the joint, if it hangs off something), turned to face you. It's an object like the box or a reference picture: **Move (G)** slides it (its blue arrow pushes it through the model), **Rotate (R)** turns it, and the arrow on the plane points to the side that stays. The view is cut open there, with the cut faces filled in solid and **red where two solids overlap** (parts that clash, or bits left inside a part). The Section view card shows three cross-sections, on the plane and square to it either way, with each part's outline, overlaps in red and the gap or overlap read out in mm. **Flip** keeps the other side, **Face the view** turns it square to where you're looking from, **At selection** puts it back through the selected parts, **Cut the view** and **Show plane** turn the cut and the plane on and off, and **Size** sets how big it's drawn and how much the cross-sections show. While the cut is on, clicks go through to what's left, so you can pick faces inside a part. **X** or **K** turns it off.
- **Orthographic view:** View › Orthographic (Numpad 5, or the Persp/Ortho button under the view cube) for a flat, no-perspective view that lines up with references.
- **Colour it in:** brush paint onto the model, fill whole parts, and switch the bare plastic between grey plastic, resins, bone, or black, grey or white primer to preview a scheme.
- **Connections you can come back to:** click a joint's marker in the view, the joint icon in the Parts list, or ⌖ in Properties to select the connection itself rather than a part. Then Move (G) drags its point and Rotate (R) turns its axes while the parts stay put, and Properties shows its type, axis and limits, offsets you can type or nudge (the point, and the part away from the point; for an extra pin or rail, each end), **Clear offsets**, and magnets at it.
- **Image to relief:** File › Image to relief… (or **Make a relief…** on a reference image) turns a picture into a solid, with no AI involved. **Outline** raises the logo's shape to an even height with an optional bevelled edge, **Depth map** uses the picture's brightness as height (light or dark raised), and **Whole picture** makes a plaque. You can set the threshold, smoothing, width, height, bevel and backing plate. **Place on a part…** puts it wherever you click on a surface; then turn, resize (across; it keeps its height) and sink it, and **Wrap to surface** bends it to follow the curve. While placing, click elsewhere on the same face (or use **Move →/↑**) to slide it along the face; **Centre on the face** puts it in the middle and **Square to the face** lines it up with the face's edges. **Repeat** makes a **Grid** of copies, or **Fill the face** with as many as cover it (gap 0 for a seamless pattern, like a checkerboard), and **Trim to the face's edges** (with an optional margin) cuts the pattern off cleanly where the face ends. **Carry on round corners** takes it across creases onto the faces next to it (as far as **Up to °** from the face you clicked), unfolded so the picture keeps its shape round a bent armour plate, with mitred corners; rivets and small bumps are left out of the unfolding and the relief sits over them. The faces it covers are outlined in blue; Shift-click faces to add them or take them out. It stays attached to that part and merges into one solid on export.
- **Scatter on a face:** select one or more parts (three different skulls, say), then **Scatter** (Modify workspace, or Modify › Scatter on a face…) and click the face of another part, a shield. Copies land at random spots over the face, mixed at random, kept apart by the **Spacing** and inside the face's edges (with an optional margin), each standing straight out of the surface where it lands and sunk in a little so it joins on export. Set **How many**, a **Size %** range, **Turn** (random, or lined up with a little wobble), **Tilt**, and which way each piece **Faces out**; **Shuffle** for a new pattern. **Carry on round corners** and Shift-clicking faces work as for reliefs. The copies share their part's mesh and are fixed to the part they're on. **Push through, out the back by** sinks each one right through the part so it pokes out of the back (a bolt through a shield).
- **Merge or cut:** when you click **Done** on a relief or a scatter, it asks whether its parts go on as **Separate parts** (fixed on, still movable), **Merge into** the part (a mesh boolean add) or **Cut into** it (a boolean subtract: their shape pressed in as deep as they sink, or holes right through when pushed through). It's a modifier either way, so you can switch later, and Apply makes it permanent.
- **Popups you can move and resize:** drag any popup card or dialog (crack, relief, placing, scatter, mirror/array, orient, link in place, section view, export, preferences, projects) by its title bar, and resize it from the grip in its bottom-right corner. Each one remembers where you left it and its size; double-click the title bar to put it back. They're kept on screen when the window shrinks.
- **Modifiers:** like Blender's modifier stack. A part keeps its mesh as it came in, and a list of modifiers worked on it in order, under Properties › **Modifiers**. Each one can be turned **on or off** (the tick), **reordered** (▲ ▼), **applied** (made part of the mesh for good, with any above it that are on) or **removed** (✕), and its settings changed right there. **Crack & cave in** shows all its settings plus **Shuffle** and **Move the hit**. A **Relief** or **Scatter** has a **Result** of Separate (its parts sit on this one), Merge (joined into the mesh) or Cut (taken away from it), and **Change placement…** opens the placing card again as it was set (Shuffle, for a scatter, re-rolls the pattern). Add one from **Add modifier…**, or by finishing a crack, relief or scatter, which now become modifiers instead of changing the mesh for good. The stack is worked out in the background, each step kept so only what changed is redone; it's saved with the project (with the mesh as it came in) and worked out again on opening, and Undo covers every change.
- **Split / break in two:** select a part, then **Split** in the Modify workspace (or Modify › Split in two…) and click where to cut it; the cut runs up the screen through that spot. It's a modifier: pick the **Break** (**Snapped**: broad angular facets, splintered; **Torn**: ragged teeth and ridges; **Melted (lance)**: a wavering burn line with drag lines, a burnt-away gap and optional **slag beads** along the edges; **Clean cut**), its **Roughness** and **Feature size**, how much is **cut away** between the halves, how far they're **pulled apart**, **Turn** and **Tilt** the cut, and **Keep** both halves or one side. **Shuffle** for a new break, **Move the cut**, or **Line up with the view** to set it across the screen again. **Apply** makes the second half a part of its own.
- **Ooze & growths:** **Ooze** in the Modify workspace (or Modify › Ooze…), then click spots on any parts, one after another, and organic ooze is strung between them: it leaves each surface with a splat, droops under its own weight, necks thin as it stretches, and swells in lumps, with pustules and drips hanging off the low points. Pick a **Look** to start from (**Thick ooze**, **Slime strings & webs**, **Lumpy growth**), then set the **Thickness**, how **Lumpy**, how much it **Sags**, how **Stretched** thin it's pulled in the middle, **Threads** (thin strings of slime across spans, along it and down to the surface, beaded) and their thickness, 1–4 **Strands** twisted round each other, **Pustules** on the strands (and their **size**), pustules **On the surface** round where it sticks (found on the part itself, so none hang off an edge), **Webbing**: sheets of goo **In the bends**, **Between strands** (which spreads them apart, a webbed rope) and **Down to the surface**, with its **reach**, **thickness**, **Web style** (a **Torn sheet**, or a **Net of cells**, most of them open, ringed by thick rims, like stretched slime) and **holes**, **Drips** and their length, the **Splat** size and the **Detail**; **Shuffle** for new lumps and drips; Backspace or **Take off last spot** to step back. It's its own part, fixed to the part it starts on, and keeps its spots on the parts they're on, so **Change ooze…** opens it again to carry it on or change it (and it follows those parts if they've moved). A closed solid, ready to merge on export. No AI: it's a blobby field made into a mesh with Manifold.
- **Burst & growth:** select a part, then **Burst** (Modify workspace, or Modify › Burst…) and click where it bursts out: a growth popping out of the hull, like a rotting tank. Round the spot the hull **swells** out (how far, and how far the swell spreads); in the middle it's **torn open** in a jagged hole with the plate round it broken into **petals** peeled up and out on hinges at their outer edge (how many, how far, how ragged, how thick the plate is); out of the hole swells a **growth** (size, how far it stands out, how lumpy) with **rings** running round it like a fungus, **pustules**, **pock holes** and curved **horns**. The petals bend back like sheet metal, curling more towards the tip and keeping the **Plate** thickness as they bend; horns curve (**Curl**). **From: Sphere inside** places a ball under where you click instead (shown see-through): set its **radius** and how **deep** its middle sits; with **Warp the part** on, the whole part round it is pushed out from the ball's middle (**Push**, how far it spreads, and **Lumpy push** for an uneven, living swell). If the ball reaches the surface it breaks through there, torn open with the growth coming out of it; deeper, it only bulges the plate. **Look** starts you from Burst open, Swelling (not broken), Overgrown or Torn open; Shuffle for a new one; click elsewhere to move it. Done keeps it as a modifier (add several for several bursts), with all its settings to change later. Only the shells of the part it reaches are worked on, so it copes with big kits (a 7.5-million-triangle tank took about half a minute). No AI: noise, a level-set growth and exact booleans with Manifold.
- **Growths that push parts aside:** **Growth** (Modify workspace, or Modify › Growth…) then click where it goes. It starts as a quick **outline**: a see-through ball (its room) with a fainter one showing how far it will bend things, fixed to the part you clicked so it moves with it. Drag it into place with the gizmo and size it (**[** and **]**, or Size); Properties lists the parts it will push. Nothing heavy happens until **Grow it**: then the lumpy growth (rings, pustules, pock holes; curved **horns** if you ask for them, their length a share of its size so they stay in proportion) is made and every part round it is pushed out of its way and bent, worked out again whenever it or they move (**Back to outline** to move it about quickly again; an outline isn't exported): put it at the corner of two plates and the corner opens up, each plate shoved its own way, with the growth in their place. **Parts are** Shoved aside (the usual) or Bulging round it; **Reach** (how far round it the parts bend), **Push**, **Sink in** (how far they bed into it) and **Lumpy push**. Any part can be a growth too (**Use as growth** in its Properties), such as a sculpted growth you imported: it starts as an outline too, and pushes with the ellipsoid that fits it (**Room %**). Each part it pushes gets a "Pushed by" modifier, to turn off, apply or remove (removed, that growth leaves that part alone). **Not a growth** puts everything back. No AI. **Connect with ooze**: Ctrl-click two or more growths and parts with bursts on them (or use the button in a growth's or burst's Properties, which takes all there are, starting from that one) and ooze is strung between them nearest to nearest, each end leaving its growth on the side that's out in the open; it's the usual ooze card, so change its looks, click more spots, Shuffle, then Done. In the ooze card, **Join up growths** strings it on through every growth and burst after the spots you've clicked.
- **Clean out insides:** select a part, then **Clean inside** in the Prepare workspace (or Prepare › Clean out insides) to keep its outside skin and nothing within it: overlapping pieces, parts buried inside others (a body under its armour, pegs, inner copies), sealed hollows and stray bits inside all go, leaving one clean model to print. Closed pieces are joined exactly with Manifold; bits that aren't closed (open sheets, broken shells) are kept only where they can be seen from outside, tested by casting rays from each one. **Keep** every outside piece or the **biggest piece only**; broken shells are mended exactly first; then **Open bits** (what still isn't closed): kept where they're outside, all removed, or, as a last resort, **shrink-wrap** it all at the **Detail** you set (re-meshes the whole surface); **Check** Quick, Normal or Thorough (Thorough keeps more of the deep crevices on a very open mesh, and takes longer). It's a modifier, showing what it removed; a big open mesh can take a minute. No AI.
- **Crack & cave in:** select a part, then **Crack** in the Modify workspace (or Modify › Crack & cave in…) and click where it got hit. Round that spot it's cracked into slabs, with jagged rings round the hit and cracks running out from it. Each slab is tipped and pushed in, deepest in the middle, like a broken sidewalk or a punched armour panel, and hairline **spider cracks** run on past the edge. Set the **Radius**, how far it **caves in**, how many **rings** and **cracks**, **crack width**, how **jagged**, how much each slab **tumbles**, and whether the middle is **caved in** or **punched through**. **Goes**: **Through the part** (the whole thickness breaks; by default the cracks stop short of the back, **Hold together**, so it stays one piece to print, or **Break right through** lets the slabs come apart), or **Surface only**: just a layer at the top breaks up, as on a thick block or a sidewalk, with a **Crack depth** (how deep the cracks cut, at most) and an **Effect depth** (how thick the layer is that breaks into slabs and caves in); under it the part stays whole. Click elsewhere to hit it there instead, **Shuffle** for new cracks. It works from the part as it was, on curved panels too. One Undo takes it back. It copes with messy meshes (pieces touching only at an edge, stray triangles, doubled faces): it cuts the solid that Merge finds, separate pieces of the part (a toe, a rivet) are cut or kept as they are, and triangles that aren't part of any closed solid go through untouched, so nothing of the part goes missing; **Split** does the same. No AI: it's a random crack pattern cut with Manifold.
- **Magnets:** pairs of magnet holes where parts meet, in common disc sizes or your own, with clearance for resin or FDM. They move with the parts and are cut into the exported files. By default (**Holes: Square to the seam**) both holes of a pair sit on one shared axis, straight across where the parts meet (taken from the average of each surface around the spot, so rivets and texture don't tip it), so the magnets go in straight and meet face to face, even if your two clicks aren't opposite each other. A hole already close by in either part is reused, so every option for one spot (left pads, swap arms) lines up with the same hole in the body, and it's cut only once. **Line up** in a magnet's row, or **Line up all** in the bar, does this to magnets you already have; **As clicked** puts each hole along the surface where you click.
- **Mirror and array:** mirror parts in place or make mirrored copies ("Right Arm" → "Left Arm", attached on the other side with its own joint), copy along a line, or round an axis through any centre: the part's own, its joint, what it's attached to, another part, or a spot you click. Copies bring everything attached to them. Ctrl+M (as in Blender), or Assemble › Mirror… / Array….
- **Alternate parts:** heads, weapons or arms for the same spot share one joint, and one shows at a time. Select them and **Make alternates** (Assemble menu or workspace, or the **Alternates** tab beside Parts); click one to show it; save the mixes you like as **builds** and export them, or every combination.
- **Model kit mode:** pieces go onto sprues (copies too, from names like `Leg x8`), packed in a box with the box art and instruction booklet from the folder. The box opens by itself as the kit comes in: the lid lifts off, the sprues and booklet come out onto the bench, and the lid goes back on the empty box.
- **Instructions:** a PDF in the folder becomes a booklet you can flip through.
- **Export… asks what and how:** **Whole model** or **Selected objects** (with or without the parts attached to them), as **One merged object** (one STL, joined into one solid when Merge is on) or **Separate objects** (one STL per part, each saved where it sits now, so they line up again when you open them together). The button says how many files you'll get; the merge, seam, pocket and glue-on options sit underneath.
- **Export for printing:** the whole model as one STL, just a selection, each part separately, or whole sprues. **Merge into one clean solid** (on by default) joins every overlapping part and piece into a single closed shell, so no surfaces are left inside to print as walls or voids, fuses pieces that only touch face to face, fills any hollow sealed inside, smooths out hairline slivers, and cuts magnet holes exactly. Small **loose bits** within 2 mm of the rest that don't touch it (a fin floating beside a barrel) are joined on with a slim bridge as thick as the bit, or kept separate or left out if you choose. **Fill seams** closes the hairline gaps between parts that only nearly touch, so they merge too. Broken shells (a sculpt exported with small holes, flaps on an edge, pinches) are **mended** in the merge, exactly where they're broken: the holes closed, the flaps dropped, the pinches split by a hair, and the rest of the surface left as it was; and every merged export has its pinches split apart, so every edge has exactly two faces (no non-manifold edges). Only open sheets with nothing behind them can't be mended: for those, **Last resort: shrink-wrap** (off by default) re-meshes the whole file as one watertight solid at the detail you set. **Glue-on pieces** (Export › Ooze, reliefs and scatters as separate glue-on pieces, or **Glue-ons** in the Export workspace): ooze and growths, reliefs and scattered parts each come out as their own STL, and the model comes out without them, even ones merged in as a modifier. So you can print a model you already have (or have already printed) and glue them on. Pick which kinds go out separately; **Shape their backs to the model** cuts the model out of each piece so it sits flush on the surface it was made on, with an optional **Glue gap**; **Glue-on pieces only** exports just them.
- **Projects save automatically** in your browser. Save a project file to back up your work or move it to another computer.

## Quick start

1. Open the page and choose **File › Open folder** (or drag a folder of STLs onto the bench).
2. Choose how they come in: **Model kit**, **Off the sprue, spread out**, or **Where the files put them**. Then a **Which parts?** screen shows every file with a picture (grouped by subfolder, with a filter, **All** and **None**): untick what you don't want and **Load**. Every part starts ticked; in a big folder (over 40 files or 200 MB) none are, so you pick. Cancel lists the files in the Files tab with nothing loaded; **Load selected** there adds more later.
3. Pick a snap tool on the left (**Face** is a good start), click the area on the part that moves, then the area it attaches to.
4. Fine-tune in the **Adjust** panel (bottom left) if needed, then **✓ Accept** (or **Enter**) to keep it and close the panel, or **Undo snap** to put it back. Press **K** for the **Section view**: it cuts through the joint so you can see how it sits.
5. **File › Export…** when you're done.

Try **File › Load sample kit** to play without your own files.

## Tools

| Tool | Key | Use it for |
|---|---|---|
| Select | `1` | Pick parts (Ctrl-click or Ctrl-drag for several) |
| Move / Rotate | `G` / `R` | Drag parts, the kit box or a whole sprue. **Axes** in the bar: the world's, the part's own, or those of what it's attached to (its joint, or its parent) |

| Magnet | `N` | Click where two parts meet for a pair of magnet holes (common sizes, or your own, with print clearance). | Colour | `C` | Brush paint or fill parts (Alt-click picks a colour, `E` erases, Alt-wheel sizes the brush) |
| Ball | `B` | Ball into socket |
| Face | `H` | Flat face to flat face, pegs into holes |
| Mid | `M` | Centre a part in a gap |
| 3-Point | `T` | Match three points on each part |
| Match | `J` | Fit shaped or notched surfaces: pick areas on the moving part, then on one or more parts it fits against, then **Fit parts** |

Each tool's settings appear in the bar under the menus while it's active.

## Getting around

- **Workspaces**, like a slicer's: tabs across the top for each stage of the job, **Ctrl+PgUp / PgDn** to step through them. Each shows its own tools down the left and its operations as small tiles at the top of the right-hand panel (hover one, or the workspace's name, for what it does); tiles that can't be used right now are hidden. The menus are grouped the same way: **File · Edit · Assemble · Modify · Prepare · View**.
  - **Files**: import STLs, open a folder, the sample kit, new / open / save projects, saved projects, reference images, import settings
  - **Assemble**: move, rotate, pose, orient and the snap tools; link in place, lay out, mirror, array, alternate parts, sprues and the box, joints, section view
  - **Modify**: crack, burst, growth, split, scatter, ooze, relief, mirror, array, paint
  - **Prepare**: clean out insides, hollow pockets, seams, magnets, drop to grid, square up, section view
  - **Export**: **Export…** (whole model or selected objects, as one merged object or separate ones), sprues, glue-ons, builds. The tab itself doesn't open anything; Export… (or Ctrl+E) does
- **Properties** shows only what's about the selected part (its size, links, pose, connections, magnets and modifiers); the operations are in the workspace tiles and menus, not repeated there. Modifiers appear once a part has one.
- **Keyboard shortcuts** follow Blender's defaults wherever the bench has the same thing, so Blender habits carry over (press **F1** or **?** for the full sheet, or the **? shortcuts** button at the bottom):
  - **Tools**: **W** select (Q works too), **G** move, **R** rotate, **P** pose, **O** orient, **C** colour (**E** erases, **[ ]** brush size), **M** magnets
  - **Snap tools**, numbered as they are down the left: **1** ball & socket, **2** face to face, **3** midpoint, **4** 3-point, **5** surface match, **6** lucky
  - **Selecting**: **A** select all, **Alt+A** deselect, **Ctrl+I** invert, **Shift**- or **Ctrl**-click to add or take away, **Ctrl**-drag to box select
  - **Parts**: **Shift+A** add a shape, **Shift+D** duplicate, **X** or **Del** delete, **F2** rename, **H** hide, **Shift+H** hide the rest, **Alt+H** show all, **Ctrl+P** attach to the active part, **Alt+P** detach, **Ctrl+M** mirror, **[ ]** a growth smaller / bigger
  - **View**: **Numpad .** (or **F**) frames the selection, **Home** (or **Shift+C**) everything; **Numpad 1 / 3 / 7** front, right, top (with Ctrl the other side), **Numpad 9** the other side, **Numpad 2 / 4 / 6 / 8** turn the view, **Numpad 5** perspective / orthographic, **Numpad /** local view, **Numpad + / −** zoom; **K** section view; **double-click** centres the view on a spot
  - **Panels**: **N** side panel, **T** toolbar, **Ctrl+Space** just the view, **Ctrl+PgUp / PgDn** previous / next workspace
  - **Files**: **Ctrl+O** open project, **Ctrl+Shift+O** saved projects, **Ctrl+S** save project, **I** import STL (Ctrl+Shift+I belongs to the browser), **Ctrl+E** export, **Ctrl+,** preferences; **Ctrl+Z / Ctrl+Shift+Z** undo and redo; **Esc** cancels, **Backspace** takes back the last pick
  - Not like Blender: **G** and **R** pick the move and rotate gizmos rather than starting a drag, there's no **S** scale (parts keep their printed size; shapes are sized in Properties), and **Ctrl+N** stays with the browser.
- **Middle-drag** (or left-drag) to orbit, **Shift+middle-drag** (or right-drag, or Shift-drag) to pan, **wheel** to zoom

## Tips

- Name files like `Leg x8.stl` or `2x Sword.stl` to bring in that many copies.
- Name files like `Pauldron mirror.stl`, `Arm_L (mirror for R).stl` or `Shin + mirror.stl` to bring in one as it is and a mirrored twin (one per copy with `x2` and the like). The twin is named for the other side where it can be (`Arm_R`), or after what it's for. Turn it off, or choose which way to mirror (X, Y or Z), under Preferences › Importing.
- A folder with an image gets a printed kit box; a folder with a PDF gets a booklet.
- Select a part and use **Swap with…** or **Make base** in Properties to change which part is the parent.
- Armour plates and pistons that ride a channel: Ball the plate's bottom ball into its socket, then Ball (or Face, or Match) its top end into the channel on the leg. Adjust shows **Slide in it**: the plate keeps its socket, turns to reach the channel, faces away from the leg, and slides up and down the channel as you pose the leg, keeping that facing (Properties › **Keep facing this way**; turn the plate itself in Pose to set a new one). **Turn on its axis** spins it if it faces the wrong way; **Slides along** changes the direction (Channel, Across, Out, the part's X/Y/Z, or **Pick faces…**); untick **Stop at the channel's ends** to let it run free. Repeat for each plate.
- Set a part's connection type in Properties. A two-piece piston: the cylinder **Pinned** to one part, the rod a **Roller** in the cylinder, and the rod's end **Connect also to…** the other part with a pin. A one-piece piston: **Pinned** at the bottom, its top end on a rail on the other part. In Link in place, contacts the tree leaves out are listed under **Also connect**: tick a piston's far end there.
- Rearrange by dragging in the Parts list (and in the Link in place review): drop a part on another part's name to attach it (with a joint where they touch), on the right end of its row to make it an option of that part, or on the strip below the list to set it loose.
- When you move a part that hangs off another, you're asked whether to bring the whole group along.
- The last piece of a puzzle? In Match, pick areas on every part it touches and it fits against them all at once.
- Feeling lucky works best on parts made to fit: cut faces, pegs, sockets and seams that sit flush. A search takes from a few seconds to a minute depending on the parts and your computer; **Quick** in the options bar is faster, **Thorough** looks harder. Its guesses are listed in Adjust, best first.
- Printing in resin? Merge already fills hollows that are sealed completely. Prepare › Check for hollow pockets also finds air whose only way out is a crack too narrow to drain, and shows it shaded red with a bold outline; tick **Fill hollow pockets** in Export to fill those too. Your parts stay as they are.
- Merging takes a moment on detailed sculpts (several seconds for half a million triangles), and the file often comes out smaller: triangles that change the shape by less than 5 microns are merged away. A piece with holes too big to close goes into the file as it was, and the export note says so.
- Match pose works from the picture's point of view: line the picture up with the figure (similar size, overlapping, square on), and use a cut-out or a plain background. One picture can't show turns toward or away from it, so check the result from the side.
- Paint is for previewing: STL files don't carry colour, so exports are plain geometry.

## Files

```
index.html       page layout: menus, panels, dialogs
css/styles.css   all the styling
js/app.js        the app: scene, tools, kit, export
js/math.js       the geometry (snapping, fitting, seams, sprues), with no page code
js/lucky.js      the "Do ya feel lucky?" search, plain number crunching
js/lucky-worker.js  runs that search in the background, one per spare processor core
js/solid.js      merging parts into one clean solid for export
js/solid-worker.js  runs the merge and relief building in the background
js/relief.js     image to relief: reads the picture, traces outlines, builds the raised solid
js/crack.js      crack & cave in: the crack pattern, cutting the slabs and pushing them in
js/remesh.js     shrink-wrap, the last resort for open sheets (distance grid, flood fill, level set)
js/grow.js       growths: the lumpy ball, and pushing the parts round it out of its way
js/burst.js      burst & growth: the swell, the torn petals and the growth (level set, horns, pustules)
js/clean.js      clean out insides: the outside skin only (union, then a sight test for open bits)
js/split.js      split: the break surface (snapped, torn, melted, clean) and cutting the part in two
js/ooze.js       ooze & growths: the strands, lumps, drips and splats, made into one closed mesh
js/vendor/       Manifold, the exact mesh-boolean library the merge uses (manifold.js and manifold.wasm)
```

Because the scripts are separate files, the page has to be served from a web address (GitHub Pages, or any local server such as `python -m http.server`). Double-clicking `index.html` to open it from disk won't run it: browsers block scripts loaded that way.

## Requirements

A recent desktop browser (Chrome, Edge, Firefox or Safari) and an internet connection the first time the page loads. It works on phones and tablets, though a mouse is much easier.

## Privacy

Everything runs in your browser. Your STL files, box art and instructions are never uploaded; projects are stored in your browser's own storage.
